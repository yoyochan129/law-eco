- 日期：2026
- 摘要：The United States maintains a regime of secrecy so expansive that even members of Congress are denied access to the information it protects. This Article is the first to examine that regime--known as confidential supervisory information (CSI). CSI, which is nondisclosed information gathered by bank supervisors when examining financial institutions, governs our nation's $25 trillion banking system. We trace CSI's origins and show how a limited norm of supervisory confidentiality hardened into a sweeping doctrine without congressional design. We conclude that the modern CSI regime is no longer legally defensible. Supervisory secrecy should be treated not as a default condition of financial regulation, but as a narrow, time-bound exception within a broader commitment to transparency, accountability, and democratic governance.
